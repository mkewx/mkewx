The detective pulled his collar tight against the bitter wind. Another subzero morning in Milwaukee, and the case was just heating up.

**"Nine below,"** he muttered, watching his breath crystallize in the amber glow of the streetlight. **"Wind chill making it feel like negative twenty-five."** The kind of cold that made your teeth ache and your eyes water before you even stepped outside.

Lake Michigan was smoking this morning—that eerie phenomenon where the **33-degree water** meets air twenty degrees colder, creating fog that rises like steam from a cauldron. From his office window on Wisconsin Avenue, he could barely make out the outline of the Hoan Bridge through the mist.

The forecast said this arctic blast would last through the weekend. **High of 12 on Saturday,** maybe climbing to **18 by Sunday** if we were lucky. The Weather Service had *winter weather advisories* posted from here to Green Bay.

But Monday—Monday was when things would get interesting. A low-pressure system moving up from the south would collide with this arctic air mass, and Milwaukee would be right in the crosshairs. **Six to nine inches of snow expected, with winds gusting to 35 mph.** Blizzard conditions weren't out of the question.

He poured another cup of coffee, black as the Milwaukee River in January. The steam rose like those lake-effect clouds, and he thought about all the stories this city's weather could tell. Tonight, the story was simple: *bundle up, Milwaukee. Winter isn't done with us yet.*
