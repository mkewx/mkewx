*Captain's Log, Stardate 2603.06. The USS Milwaukee has entered a turbulent region of atmospheric space near the Lake Michigan Corridor. Sensors indicate a massive precipitation anomaly approaching from the south-southeast.*

"Ensign, report."

"Captain, long-range sensors are picking up a significant weather disturbance. It's... it's big, sir."

"How big?"

"**Rain probability at 90%.** We're looking at **sustained SSE winds of 10 to 15 miles per hour.** Possible thunderstorm activity by late afternoon. Barometric pressure has dropped to **29.3 inches** and falling."

The captain gripped the arms of his chair. He'd piloted this city through plenty of rough weather before, but something about this system felt different. It felt warm.

"Temperature readings?"

"That's the unusual part, sir. We're registering a high of **56°F.** Possibly **62°F** in the downtown corridor."

A murmur ran through the bridge crew. Sixty-two degrees. In early March. In Milwaukee. The science officer raised an eyebrow.

"Fascinating. This thermal event is approximately **twenty degrees above historical norms** for this stardate. The atmosphere appears to be... confused."

"Confused?"

"That is the technical term, Captain."

The viewscreen flickered. Thick clouds rolled across the lakefront like a gray curtain being drawn across a stage. Rain began to streak the windows of every coffee shop on Brady Street. Umbrellas went up along Water Street like tiny deflector shields.

"Captain, we're getting reports from the surface. **New rainfall amounts between a quarter and half an inch expected.** Some neighborhoods are reporting puddles in excess of... well, a lot of puddles, sir."

"Red alert?"

"More of a... yellow umbrella alert, sir."

The captain stood. He walked to the viewscreen and watched the rain come down on his city. Somewhere out there, people were running between buildings, holding newspapers over their heads, making choices they'd regret about footwear.

"All hands," he said into the comm. "This is your captain speaking. We're heading into a warm front with heavy rain and the possibility of thunder. **Temperatures will hold near 52°F overnight.** The worst of it hits this afternoon through the evening hours. I want everyone in waterproof boots and I want every umbrella on this ship deployed."

"Aye, Captain."

"And Ensign? One more thing."

"Sir?"

"Don't let anyone tell you **56 degrees in March** isn't worth celebrating. Even if it comes with rain. That's practically spring in Sector 414."

*End log.*

*The forecast is out there, Milwaukee. It's wet, it's warm, and it doesn't care about your hair.*
